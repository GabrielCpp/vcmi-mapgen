# core/steps/terrain_gen/

## Map

- `model.py`: the `TerrainModel` role `TerrainStep` is given, with `TerrainOptions` in and `TerrainDraw` out. `cli/steps.py` builds the places model, the only one, from a surface form. `SURFACE_FORMS` names the surface forms per water mode, and `DEFAULT_WATER` is `topology`.
- `places.py`: `PlacesTerrain`, the default model: the place-first surface over today's underground. `surface` runs shapes first: the `SurfaceForm` gives the land, `shapes.py` grows the place shapes on it, `homes.py` chooses the homes, the territories and their doors stand on the grown shapes, the form floods its water, `roles.py` labels the roles, and the palette partition, identity, a base coat and Paint follow. `carried` hands each place the reconcile left its territory before `bordered` draws the final doors. Once the places have grown, it splits them into territories, about as many as a corpus map of the nearest player count and land holds, less the dull neutral ones that join a neighbour, walls every border between two territories except at their doors, and plans the passages: each door 1 tile wide, an inner gated passage 3 tiles wide on the surface and 1 tile wide underground. The underground runs the same front with no home: `underground_places` calls `bordered` with no player, so its territory count comes from the corpus level 1 records and its doors follow the same hop rule. Before the underground draws its doors, `plan_gates` in `gate_sites.py` plans the Subterranean Gates, as many as `priors.gates` draws for the map. Each gate counts as one door hop on both levels: the underground draws its doors with the surface behind the gates, and the surface widens its walls again with the underground behind them. `PlaceMap.gates` publishes the gates as `PlannedDoor` values, surface side first.
- `coastline.py`: the `SurfaceForm` role. `land` returns a `Coastline`: the land the places grow on, the land count the place count reads, and its log lines. `flood` takes a `Shore`, the grown shapes with their territories, doors, wall widths and the tiles that stay land, and returns the layout with water labelled -1. `NoiseForm` is the variant behind `--water-mode none`, `normal` and `islands`: a noise land mask from `macro.py` first, and no flood after.
- `water.py`: `TopologyForm`, the default variant behind `--water-mode topology`. Its land is all land. Its flood comes after the territories: `walled_fronts` lists the touching territory pairs no door joins, and `water_on_walls` carves each as a band half its wall width deep on both sides, widest wall first, then the edge water along noise arcs of the map ring, deepening them in turn toward one corpus map's water share. `WaterTarget.masses` is the count of walled fronts it carves first. The land stays one piece, each place keeps one piece with half its tiles or more, and the home rooms and gate sites stay land. The underground plans its passages one tile wide and drops tunnel protection on place fronts, so vegetation walls each front except its passage. Its terrain log line reports the drawn and realised palette region counts, the same-terrain share of place borders and the raw cut.
- `paint.py`: Paint, which grades a transition band across each border between places of different dominant terrain, grows accents inside each place, textures each place's interior with the tables counted inside corpus places, and keeps every place's dominant share at the corpus floor through the tiler's erosion.
- `place_draws.py`: the corpus draws a places map starts from: the place count, the role mix and target sizes the shapes grow to, and `pair_affinity`, how much more often two roles border in the corpus than at random.
- `shapes.py`: `shape_caps`, the corpus place count and size mix for the land, `spread_anchors`, anchors spread farthest-first over the largest land mass, and `grow_shapes`, the regions grown from them. It never retries.
- `homes.py`: `choose_homes`, the home places chosen farthest-first by walking distance among the places that fit a town room and hold no more than the home budget while enough of them do, accepted once no two homes touch or share a neighbour and they stand the corpus home gap apart, else the best of `HOME_DRAWS` draws.
- `roles.py`: `label_roles`, the role of every non-home place by a Metropolis walk over the realised adjacency with the corpus role-pair affinity and a size score from the corpus size fractions. A pocket labels only a place with one neighbour, a pass only a place beside another territory.
- `layout.py`: `Layout`, the label grid of the places, and `grow`, the regions grown from their anchors to their target sizes with every land mass no anchor reached as a pocket, plus the room tests (`roomy`, `home_gap`).
- `palette.py`: the palette partition: the corpus palette region count for the place count and land, and `group_places`, which grows that many regions of whole places, each connected on the realised place adjacency, to corpus terrain-blob capacities.
- `identity.py`: one dominant terrain per palette region, drawn by a Metropolis walk over the regions from the corpus terrain of their places' roles and the macro terrain pair table, with two adjacent regions never on one terrain. A region holding a home takes a town terrain. It returns each place's dominant.
- `place_map.py`: the place map a flood fill reads, the adjacency a label grid realises, and the repair once despeckle changed the grid. The flood gates every pair, with the entrance plan over every pair as its passages, its bands as wide as the caller asks. `front_cells` lists the tiles beside another place.
- `border_kinds.py`: the kind of each realised place pair (map-math 7). `draw_kinds` keeps a spanning forest of the realised pairs passable, weighted by how often the corpus leaves the role pair passable, and draws the rest from `PlaceStats.adjacency` by role pair.
- `territories.py`: the territories of a place map. `draw_territory_record` draws one corpus map near the map's player count and land, for its territory count and its home share. `home_budget` turns that share into the tiles each home territory grows toward. `draw_partition` grows the homes in turns, the smallest first, each by a random free neighbouring place that closes more of the budget than it overshoots. `group_neutral` groups the other places into neutral territories the same way, each toward an even share of the land the homes leave over the territories the record has left. `merge_dull` then folds every neutral territory under the record's `prize_tiles` (its land per prize) into the neighbour it shares the longest border with, smallest first, and leaves the ids gapped. `label_borders` in `place_map.py` counts those borders. `settle` splits off every piece a territory lost as the places grew. `draw_doors` opens a spanning forest of the territories over the realised pairs with one door per edge. `detour_doors` then adds a door on every touching territory pair more than `MAX_HOP` doors apart or not joined at all. Each door takes a second door at the corpus odds. `unreached` names the territories no door path joins to a home. `wall_kinds` closes every other territory border, and `territory_plan` publishes each door with its tiles. `wall_depths` gives every zone pair of two territories its wall width from the door hops between them: 1 tile one door apart, 3 two apart and 4 three or more apart or cut off at side 72, each growing with the map side. `LevelPlaces.depths` publishes the widths, and the vegetation border sealer closes every open pair of the two zones that sits within that width. The underground has no home, so every territory there is neutral. `Across` holds the other level as hop links: each gate joins its zone here to its territory there, and the other level's doors join its territories. `draw_doors` and `wall_depths` take those links, so a gate counts as one door hop. No link counts as a door or a loop of the level.
- `streams.py`: `stream(seed, *parts)`, one stably seeded `Random` per part of a draw.
- `despeckle.py`: merges terrain patches too small or too thin for the tile art into the land around them.
- `gate_sites.py`: the Subterranean Gate sites, carved open on both levels and tunnelled to the nearest cavern, and `plan_gates`, the gates planned on them and on the rest of the land. `GateGround` accepts an anchor when the gate's room, its footprint, its approach and the ring around them, lies in one zone on each level and off every place front. Each zone on either level holds one gate at most, and the gates keep the corpus spacing floor. The vegetation step keeps each planned gate's room free of trees, and the gameplay step places the planned gates first.
- `levels.py`: `segment_places`, one zone per place with its sliver warnings, and `level_accents`, each level's accent patches.
- `macro.py`: the macro zone growth: the planned water mask, zones and corridors, drawn from `MacroStats`.
- `result.py`: `TerrainGrids`, the tunnel cells, `PlaceMap`, each level's places, their transition bands, the kind of each border, its `Passages` and its `TerritoryPlan` with each `PlannedDoor`, the planned Subterranean Gates, `Segmentation`, and `Accents`, each level's accent patches, all of which `TerrainStep` publishes.
- `step.py`: `TerrainStep`, which draws both levels from its `TerrainModel` and segments each level into its places.
- `texture.py`: the boundary texturing, which samples the corpus Markov tables in a band around zone borders.
