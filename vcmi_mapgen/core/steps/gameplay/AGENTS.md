# core/steps/gameplay/

## Map

- `step.py`: `GameplayStep` and the order it places objects in. It reads the `ContentPlan` and the `Accents` with `ctx.get`: a planned home's town stands with `HOME_FOOTING`. Right after the player towns it keeps each one's walk out of its home zone with `home_ways`, so no guard stands on it. Right after the shipyards it stands each player town's own sawmill and ore pit, and after the map-wide pass of `placer.py` it warns about any town with no sawmill or ore pit within 12 tiles.
- `landmark.py`: the accent landmarks, drawn from the kinds in `core/planning/landmarks.py`. Each accent patch in the `Accents` of 20 tiles or more takes one gold mine, abandoned mine, permanent stat building or dwelling, its doors on the patch. The patch the homes reach last, in travel days, takes a dragon dwelling behind a level-7 guard instead. The landmarks run right after the towns and the shipyards.
- `allocate.py`: the mine promise, a target the step log warns about rather than a gate. Before and after the map-wide pass, `keep_promise` stands the basic mines still missing so every player reaches each basic resource within 14 hero-days. Wood and ore get one mine per late player, within 3 days of the other players. The rare mines stand as the fewest that bring every player within 14 days of each rare resource: the rare resources pick in turn, so no spot holds two, each picking the offer that reaches the most players still out, then the least gap between them, then the days nearest the rare target, and a pick that later picks make redundant is dropped before any mine stands. Every further rare mine is a spaced slot of the map-wide pass. Each promised mine draws its guard from its family's corpus record, never above level 1 for wood and ore and level 3 for a rare mine. A resource that leaves a player out steps its guard down to no guard, then warns. `keeps_even` says whether a mine leaves no new player behind and the gap no wider. The map is read again after each resource or round that stood a mine. After the map-wide pass the second call stands a mine only for a player the fill cut off. `GameplayStep` then provides `PromisedWays`, which every later step keeps open.
- `placer.py`: the map-wide pass over the neutral towns, mines, dwellings, banks and visitables. `Placement.plan` turns the quota into slots, and `Placement.place` stands them and tallies the shortfall. The neutral towns stand first, each town's own pair right after them, then every other slot. Each pair mine that stood takes one slot off the resource mines, so the curve still counts every resource mine. Once the towns and their pairs stand, the roster draws the kind of each spaced slot, and every zone then holds a share of the mines, dwellings, banks and visitables proportional to its tiles. A slot that would overflow the share of every zone stands in the zone with room for it, and the overflow is logged.
- `supply.py`: `stand_pairs`, one sawmill and one ore pit per town with its entrance within 12 tiles of the town's, stood before any other mine near it. A town keeps no gap around its body, so the pair may stand right against it. Its sprite may hang outside its zone, as a town's does. The pair stays outside the quota and the round robin.
- `gold.py`: `GoldSpot`, the tiles a gold mine may stand on: past 8 days from every home through its own guard, and where the first player to reach it holds it by at most 3 days over its nearest enemy. Among those tiles a gold mine is drawn by the spacing weight of its kind, a tile where it would stand crowded last. Gold slots serve no player and stand last among the mines. Where no such tile takes one, the gold mine stands on the far land past the contest bound and the step warns. An accent landmark draws a gold mine only on a tile `GoldSpot` admits.
- `quota.py`: how many objects of each family the whole map holds, times `--density`. The towns and the resource mines follow their corpus curves over land area and player count, the resource mines less each town's sawmill and ore pit. The weekly producers take the mine rate per tile times their corpus share. Every other purpose follows its corpus rate per tile.
- `bands.py`: the slot of each object: the effort band it serves and its target in days, and the order the slots are placed in. `BandPlan.shared` splits a family over the bands by its corpus effort, map-wide. No slot serves a player. A neutral town and a gold mine take no band either.
- `roster.py`: `roster`, the kind of every spaced slot, drawn before siting so each kind's count on the finished map is known. A slot draws its kind by the chance of each kind on each site's terrain, weighed by the site's share of the tiles, and a kind that already stands or was drawn before weighs less. It returns the count of each kind: the copies standing plus the slots drawn.
- `spacing.py`: `SpotWeight`, the weight of a spot for a kind. It reads the normalised distance u from the spot to the nearest copy of the kind on its level, with the kind's count on a level taken as its share of the land, and weighs the spot by the corpus law of u over the density of random placement, never below `FLOOR`. It also says whether the spot would stand crowded, under the corpus tenth percentile of u.
- `reach.py`: `Reach`, the objects of each family each player reaches within each band, and how far one more would spread the players.
- `siting.py`: where each slot stands. It records each slot's ranking. Each object first draws its own guard from its family's corpus record through `GuardDraw`, and Siting prices every site tile once under that guard. An object of no player is drawn over the whole map, nearest its band first, in a weighted random order of the open tiles where a tile weighs the spacing weight of its kind there, in zones that still have room. A tile where the kind would stand closer to a copy than the corpus tenth percentile ranks with the tiles one band further out. A rare mine ranks by the spread, then by whether its player arrives first, then by its player's band and target. A slot tries a tile that would spread the players beyond one only after every other tile. A wood, ore or rare mine in the map-wide pass takes the lowest guard from its draw up to its promised level that keeps the players even on that resource, so no player gets a cheap repeat the others lack. A neutral town ranks by the spread, then by its gap to the nearest town on its level or to the map edge. It never stands on a tile that would spread the players beyond one, and only where 238 tiles of land lie within 12 tiles, the corpus tenth percentile, so its sawmill and ore pit have room. Every object tries every group in rank order, so it fills every site of its band before it drops to a worse one. Any object may stand in the field, with no closed tile on or around its sprite, when no snug or sided spot takes it. A large object, and any object in the open, tries the most open ground of a group first, so the fallback fills wide empty fields before it crowds an edge. `TileGrid` takes each tile's worst code within a few tiles on plain arrays, so the tests draw their grids in ASCII.
- `pick.py`: `Picker`, which object of a family stands on a site's terrain. `kinds` gives each kind's chance on a terrain for the roster, and `pick` draws only a sprite of the kind the roster named. It keeps where each kind stands.
- `economy.py`: the mine variants a terrain shows and the dwellings tied to a town.
- `result.py`: `GameplayResult`, each zone's placed objects and remaining tiles with the
  `Shortfall` of the slots no site took and the `SlotRanking` of each slot of the map-wide pass, the first tiles of the group it stood in with their sort keys, the groups it walked with each outcome (`GroupStep`), and where it stood with the centre that won, and `GateResult`, `TownsIndex` and `PromisedWays`,
  which `GameplayStep` publishes.
- `gate_pairs.py`: the Subterranean Gate pairs placed against the vegetated field, and the spread that keeps them apart. The gates the terrain model planned in `PlaceMap.gates` go first, each kept in its planned zone on both levels, and their count sets the map's gate count. Without a plan the count comes from the corpus curve.
- `shipyards.py`: which shipyard anchors are legal and which one each shore gets. A shipyard counts only when a hero boards its boat water from a walkable tile beside it. `place_link` stands one more shipyard on a player's land for a `Link`.
- `sea_links.py`: `SeaLinks`, the land each player walks and the seas it boards and lands from. Right after the shipyards, `GameplayStep` gives each player apart from a rival by land a shipyard whose sea lands on that rival's land, and warns when none fits. `sea_way` is the way a hero walks and sails to a rival town. `PromisedWays` keeps its land tiles open, so later guards and objects leave every landing free.
